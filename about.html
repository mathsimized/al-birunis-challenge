About the competition

A mathematics competition about more than answers

Al-Biruni’s Challenge 2026 is a free, nationwide mathematics competition for school students across Pakistan, organised by Mathsimized.

The idea

Think · Explain · Create

Most mathematics competitions measure how quickly and accurately a student can produce a correct answer. That matters, but it is not the whole of mathematics.

Al-Biruni’s Challenge is built around a different progression. A student first thinks — recalling, reasoning and solving under pressure. Then they explain — taking a mathematical idea and communicating it so that someone else can understand it. Finally they create — building a physical model that demonstrates a mathematical solution to a real-world problem.

Each stage is harder to fake than the last. An answer can be guessed; a clear explanation cannot. A demonstration cannot be faked at all.

The name

Al-Biruni (973–1050 CE) was a Persian polymath who measured the Earth with extraordinary precision, wrote on astronomy, geography, mathematics and pharmacology, and insisted on evidence rather than assertion. The competition takes its name from that tradition of measurement, evidence and scholarship.

What the competition tests

Skills, not just scores

Mathematical knowledge

Core understanding of the mathematics appropriate to each category, and the ability to apply it rather than merely recall it.

Number sense

Judging magnitude, spotting structure, estimating sensibly and working accurately under time pressure.

Speed & accuracy

Round 1 is a rapid-fire qualifier, so fluency and precision are tested directly.

Critical thinking

Choosing an approach, checking a result and recognising when a method is not working.

Communication

Round 2 asks a student to teach a mathematical idea clearly, in their own structure and words.

Originality & application

The Grand Finale rewards a genuine physical demonstration of a mathematical solution to a real problem.

Organisation

Run by Mathsimized

Al-Biruni’s Challenge is organised by Mathsimized, the mathematics learning platform. The competition is a separate experience with its own website, portal and administration, while students sign in with the same Mathsimized account they already use.

Free to participate There is no entry fee, no Round 2 fee, and no charge for certificates, materials or attendance at the Grand Finale.
